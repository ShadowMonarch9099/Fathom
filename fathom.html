The problem

Curiosity dies at the wall of text

Today

A student searches “deep sea creatures”. The first result is an encyclopedia article: thousands of words, citations, taxonomy tables. They skim two paragraphs and close the tab.

The pelagic zone consists of the water column of the open ocean and can be further divided into regions by depth. The epipelagic zone extends from the surface to around 200 metres. Below it the mesopelagic zone extends to around 1,000 metres, where the bathypelagic zone begins, continuing to about 4,000 metres, followed by the abyssopelagic zone extending to about 6,000 metres, and the hadopelagic zone, found within oceanic trenches. Organisms of the pelagic zone are classified according to their trophic position, distribution, and the extent of vertical migration undertaken. Nekton are distinguished from plankton by the ability to swim against currents. The mesopelagic is characterised by a dysphotic light regime in which irradiance declines exponentially with depth…
With Fathom

The same student presses Start the dive. Within ten seconds they are underwater. Depth is the progress bar, darkness is the lesson about light, and every fact arrives one card at a time, at the moment they meet the animal it describes.

Every zone boundary, pressure reading and creature in the prototype above is real. The player learns the structure of the ocean by moving through it.

Show, then tell

The player sees the anglerfish’s lure glowing in the dark before the card explains it.

One fact at a time

A discovery card holds one fact, at most 25 words. The rest unlock through more observation.

Depth is the progress bar

Going deeper is the reward, the challenge and the curriculum all at once.

Strictly 2D

Side-view cross-section of the water column. Every system is designed to read on a flat plane.

Core loop

Dive, discover, surface, go deeper

Each dive takes 3 to 6 minutes, so the game fits into a class break. A dive ends when the player rises back to the surface, which keeps a natural rhythm of exploring and then reflecting.

The world

Five zones, one vertical map

The ocean is laid out as one continuous vertical cross-section. Real zones differ hugely in thickness: the sunlight zone is 200 m deep while the midnight zone spans 3,000 m. To keep pacing even, the depth scale is compressed per zone. Each zone gets a similar amount of screen distance, and the depth meter always shows true metres.

Feature set

Everything that turns facts into play

Priority tags: MVP ships first, V1 follows, Later is for after launch. Features marked ● IN PROTOTYPE already work in the demo above. Each feature notes what it teaches.

A. Discovery and learning

How facts reach the player.

Scan to discover

MVP

Stay close to a creature while it is lit and a ring fills. When it completes, the creature is logged and a card appears with one fact.

Rewards patience and observation instead of clicking.

● IN PROTOTYPE

Three levels of knowledge

MVP

Sighted: silhouette and name. Scanned: one fact. Studied: scan it on 3 dives or watch a behaviour to unlock the full card (diet, size, depth range, conservation status).

Spaced, repeated exposure without a single extra paragraph.

Field journal

MVP

A collection book. Undiscovered entries show a silhouette and the depth to search, so every gap is a goal. Filter by zone, diet or “glows in the dark”.

Turns the zone structure into a checklist the player wants to finish.

● IN PROTOTYPE

Behaviour moments

V1

Wait near a creature and something happens: the anglerfish twitches its lure and snaps up a fish, the Atolla flashes its alarm ring when a predator comes close.

Facts become things the player watched happen.

Size compare

V1

Every journal card draws the animal to scale beside the sub and a 1.7 m diver silhouette. A sperm whale fills the panel. An amphipod is a speck.

Size is hard to grasp from numbers alone.

Co-pilot radio

V1

A sonar operator character comments in one line at key moments (“Pressure here could crush a soda can flat.”). Optional voice with captions.

Context arrives when the player is paying attention.

B. Exploration mechanics

How the ocean itself becomes the lesson.

Sunlight falloff and headlights

MVP

The water darkens with depth. Below 120 m the headlights switch on and only a cone in front of the sub is visible. Creatures behind the sub disappear into the dark.

Light disappears quickly with depth. Animals here rely on other senses.

● IN PROTOTYPE

Live instrument panel

MVP

Depth, pressure in atmospheres, water temperature and percentage of sunlight left, all updated in real time with real values.

Players absorb the numbers just by glancing at the gauges.

● IN PROTOTYPE

Sonar ping

V1

Press Space to send a ring outward. Unlogged creatures within range flash as blips for two seconds. A short cooldown stops spamming.

Echolocation, the same idea dolphins and sperm whales use.

● IN PROTOTYPE

Hull pressure rating

MVP

Each hull has a rated depth. Go past it and the screen creaks, cracks spread over the HUD, and the sub is pushed back up. This is a warning and never a fail state.

Pressure rises by about 1 atmosphere every 10 m.

Battery

V1

Headlights and sonar drain the battery. It recharges at the surface. Running low dims the lights, which pushes the player to plan the dive.

No sunlight means no free energy in the deep.

Thermocline and currents

Later

A shimmering band where temperature drops fast. Horizontal current bands drift the sub sideways and carry plankton with them.

The ocean is layered and always moving.

C. Ecosystem systems

Connecting the animals to each other.

Food web builder

V1

A journal screen where logged creatures are cards. The player draws arrows for “who eats whom”. Correct chains earn research points. A finished web for a zone unlocks a badge.

Animals depend on each other across zones.

Day and night dives

V1

Choose a day or night dive at the dock. At night, lanternfish, krill and squid rise into the sunlight zone, so some creatures can only be logged at night.

The largest daily migration on Earth, known as diel vertical migration.

Marine snow

V1

Drifting particles fall past the sub. A mission asks the player to follow the snow down and find which animals feed on it.

How food from the surface reaches the deep (the biological carbon pump).

● IN PROTOTYPE (visual)

Set-piece habitats

V1

Hand-built places on the side walls: a coral reef and kelp forest near the surface, a hydrothermal vent field with tube worms, a whale fall that changes over repeat visits, and the trench floor.

Each habitat feeds itself differently: sunlight, chemicals or a carcass.

Symbiosis pairs

Later

Log both partners (clownfish and anemone, anglerfish and its glowing bacteria) to unlock a pair card that explains what each one gets.

Cooperation in nature.

D. Progression and rewards

Reasons to dive again.

Research points and the hangar

MVP

Each discovery earns research points (RP). Spend them on hull ratings, brighter lights, a longer battery, sonar range and a camera.

The upgrade list mirrors real deep-sea vehicle engineering.

● RP COUNTER IN PROTOTYPE

Depth gates

MVP

Hull upgrades unlock zones in order. Each new zone feels earned, and it opens with a short banner that explains what makes it different.

Players learn the zones in the order they appear.

● ZONE BANNERS IN PROTOTYPE

Expeditions

V1

Short goals offered at the dock: “Log 3 animals that make their own light”, “Reach the vent field”, “See a sperm whale below 800 m”.

Each expedition quietly targets one concept.

Badges

MVP

Twilight Diver, Midnight Explorer, Hadal Pioneer, Glow Collector, Full Food Web. Reaching Challenger Deep gets its own moment: fewer people have been there than have been to space.

Milestones match real scientific achievements.

Photo mode

Later

Freeze the scene, frame a shot, and the caption fills in with the creature’s name and a fact. Save the image locally or share it.

Makes the game spread through shared images.

E. Recall

Checking what stuck, without it feeling like a test.

Surface debrief

MVP

When the sub reaches the surface: deepest point, zones reached, new species, and one three-choice question about something the player saw on this dive.

Retrieval practice at the moment memory is fresh.

● IN PROTOTYPE

“Who am I?” silhouettes

V1

A clue and three silhouettes. Pick the right one for bonus RP.

Links shape to name.

Spaced repetition

Later

Missed questions come back in later debriefs at growing intervals, all stored locally.

Moves facts into long-term memory.

F. Conservation

Showing that the ocean is under pressure too.

Debris pickup

V1

Plastic bags drift in the sunlight zone and look a lot like moon jellies. Scan one by mistake and the card explains that sea turtles make the same mistake. Collecting debris earns RP.

Plastic pollution, shown in a way players remember.

Ghost nets and bleaching

Later

A ghost net mission (cut free trapped animals) and a reef that the player sees healthy, then bleached on a warm-water dive.

Human impact, without lecturing.

Status chips

V1

Each studied card shows IUCN status (Endangered, Vulnerable, Least Concern, Data Deficient).

We still don’t know much about many deep-sea species, which “Data Deficient” makes clear.

G. Access and reach

Making sure anyone can play.

Controls for every device

MVP

WASD and arrow keys, hold-to-steer on touch screens, gamepad support through the Gamepad API.

Technical architecture

Frontend only, built on 2D canvas

Recommended stack

Vite + TypeScript + HTML5 Canvas 2D, with no game engine. The game is one camera moving over a vertical map, so an engine adds weight without much benefit. If the team prefers an engine, Phaser 3 is the best alternative.

UI (HUD, cards, journal, hangar) is plain DOM layered over the canvas. That keeps text sharp, selectable and accessible to screen readers.

Saving uses localStorage with a version number in the save data. No backend and no login: progress stays on the player’s device.

Audio uses Howler.js. Each zone has an ambient loop that crossfades with depth, getting quieter and lower deeper down.

Hosting is any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Vercel). A service worker can make it installable as a PWA that works offline in classrooms.

Render layers, back to front

L1Sky, sun, clouds, birds · screen space, slow parallax
L2Water gradient by depth, light rays · colour sampled from depth
L3Side walls, habitats, far silhouettes · parallax 0.5
L4Creatures, submarine, bubbles · world space
L5Darkness mask · offscreen canvas, headlight cone cut out with destination-out
L6Bioluminescence · drawn above the mask with additive blending
L7Scan rings, labels, sonar · world space
DOMHUD, banners, cards, journal, menus

Performance. Reuse particle objects instead of creating new ones, only update creatures near the camera, size the canvas for devicePixelRatio, and keep one offscreen canvas for the lighting mask. The prototype runs at 60 fps on a mid-range phone using these methods.

Art. The prototype draws everything with code. For production, use one illustrated style: flat 2D side-view sprites in a single sprite atlas, with a separate glow layer for each bioluminescent animal so the lighting pass can draw it on top of the dark.
